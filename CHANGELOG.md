# Changelog

Format based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), versions following [SemVer](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **The map is bilingual, English and French.** The language is resolved by `?lang=`, then the visitor's saved choice, then the browser's languages, then English. A two-state toggle in the bar carries the code of the language it switches **to**; it does not reload the page, since the map is a tool one has already set up — period, filters, zoom, selection — and a reload would throw all that away. A `?lang=` is not remembered: it is a variant of the URL, not a choice, or a shared link would change the default language of whoever follows it. Labels derived from the data are translated too: `countryNames` and `results.labels` carry both languages, and `npm run validate` requires both. English country names are the ISO 3166 short names, the usual form being kept where ISO gives a long administrative one. The settled wordings carry over without drift: "last known appearance", never "retired"; "no professional game on record", never "no team". League names are proper nouns and are not translated.
- **Deep link `?q=`**: it opens the map with a player or a team already selected, through the same search as the bar — code, full name, short label and period handle all work.
- **Guided tour.** Eight steps in three acts — reading the map (the grid, a player's thread, the legend), questioning it (selection and detail panel, a year, Isolate), thinning it out (the appearances threshold, search) — then a recap of the remaining gestures. Each step performs the gesture on the real map, then invites the visitor to do it again, without ever waiting for the right click. It opens on the first visit and replays from the `?` button; the map's state is saved and restored on exit, even when closed mid-step. Six steps below 700 px wide, hovering having no touch equivalent. Reduced motion is respected, and keyboard focus stays in the card.
- **About panel**, a third drawer next to Stats: the method in three sentences, a link to the sources, the gestures recap, the code and data licences, the repository, and the notice that this is a fan project not affiliated with Riot Games.
- **Reporting a problem takes two clicks from the map.** Two links in the About panel — display bug, data error, the latter reminding that a source is required — open a GitHub issue pre-filled with a deep link to the current selection, the browser, screen size, language, period, filters, hidden columns, isolation and zoom. The pinned team and the isolated edition fill the "Team and year" field. The issue templates and the pull request template are in English, and say that French is welcome.
- **Link previews.** A link to the map shared on Reddit, Discord, X or Slack now comes with a 1200×630 image — the map itself, Fnatic pinned and isolated — and its title and description (`og:image`, `og:url`, `og:locale:alternate`, `twitter:card summary_large_image`). A link to the site's root gets the same card.
- **Visits are counted with Cloudflare Web Analytics**: no cookies, no personal data, said in the About panel. It is the site's only third-party call, and nothing on the map depends on it.
- **League name and geographic area when hovering a region pill.** The drawer's pills showed only an acronym — `LCK`, `GPL`, `LCL`, `CBLOL` — without saying what it is or where it is played. Their tooltip now has three lines: the league's name, its area, then the column count at the current appearances threshold, which used to fill it alone. The **name** comes from the source, not from general knowledge — `Leagues.League`, queried by `League_Short`, all 14 acronyms found — through a `leagueNames` table in `data/teams.json`, collected again and compared with `/data` in § 15 of the collection report; `npm run validate` requires one per region. These are the leagues' names **today**, and the label stays plain for that reason: a family of columns spans sixteen years, the LEC having been called "EU LCS" for half its life, and a row's region is decided by the year of its edition. The **area** is derived from `homeCountries`, with no collection at all: `Leagues.Region` arrives in the same query but is circular for the LMS ("LMS"), approximate for the LCP, and would contradict the map for the LPL, where it says "China" when `homeCountries` counts Taiwan and Hong Kong as domestic — the tooltip would have belied the "imported" badge shown a few pixels away. Up to three countries are spelled out; beyond that, a short settled label, and there are only three: "Europe", "post-Soviet space", "Latin America".
- **Team names in "Unchanged rosters" lead to their column.** In the statistics drawer, player names were buttons and team names dead text: one read "GEN 2016–2017, 2 years" with no way to go and look at the row. A click now pins the team and centres the map on its column, framed on the editions it played within the displayed period. The click mode chosen in the filters — "Full careers" or "Arrivals and departures" — is kept: it is not for the shortcut to decide. **A team that has no column at that moment is greyed out and inert**, with the reason in its `title` — region unticked, below the appearances threshold, outside the displayed period, or column hidden. Nothing is unhidden behind the visitor's back: a click in the statistics has no business changing the filters without saying so. The wording is that of the drawer's checklist, both refusals stating the same rule.
- **The bar searches teams as much as players.** A team could only be found by opening the filters and reading a checklist of 120 entries grouped by region. It can now be searched by its **code**, its **full name** or its **short label** — `fnatic`, `FNC`, `pain`, `kabum` —, regardless of accents and case; picking it does the same as clicking its header. The native `<datalist>` was replaced by a hand-written combobox: it can neither present two categories, nor display anything but text, nor be styled. The combobox brings the two groups, filtering, keyboard navigation (arrows, Enter, Escape), `aria-activedescendant` and closing on an outside click. Both spellings of a renamed player remain searchable — a data rule, not a convenience —, and the line of a period handle now carries the canonical spelling alongside, so that the link between the two shows. A team with no column at that moment stays listed but inert, with its reason: finding it is already an answer, and a search result has no business changing the filters without saying so. Inert entries are skipped by the arrows — stopping on a line where Enter does nothing would make the keyboard feel broken.
- **Isolate: keep only the columns that matter.** Two gestures, two reductions, which **add up** just as the three filters already add up with each other. Clicking a **year** on the axis keeps only that edition's teams, plus those its players came from and went to — **from 19 to 40 columns out of 120** depending on the edition. An edition without its two neighbours only lists rosters: the map is about transfers, and the neighbourhood is what carries the movement. The two neighbouring rows therefore stay, under a veil, showing only those arrivals and departures. Clicking a neighbour moves the isolation there — stepping from edition to edition — and clicking the isolated edition leaves it. The **Isolate** button of the detail panel keeps only the columns the selection passed through: **1 to 7** for a player (median 1), up to **27** for a team in "Full careers" and 16 in "Arrivals and departures" — the click mode chosen in the filters decides. The point is not only to show fewer: the remaining columns are **packed together**, where a player's could be thousands of pixels apart. The map therefore re-centres on the selection after each render, and on the way back too — remembering the previous view was ruled out, since the selection may have changed in between.
- **Hovering and a plain click still only highlight**, without re-laying out anything: isolating is a distinct gesture. The button lives in the detail panel, the only place both selections share — a player is picked on the map, a team in the header — and the only place where the hand does not move while the columns rearrange. Leaving isolation keeps the selection; the × releases it; Escape, at the head of the existing cascade, does both. Selecting someone else **carries** the isolation over to them, without leaving it, and the statistics drawer's shortcuts therefore judge their refusal on the map **as it will be after** the gesture: a team excluded by the current isolation stays reachable, since the click will bring it in. Nothing is hidden on the sly: the period and the filters stay where they were set, and **a gesture that would leave no column is greyed out with its reason** rather than refused after the fact — 35 players out of 749 have no column in the period open by default, and more than half of the player × edition pairs do not combine. All 11,235 combinations were checked: the greying refuses exactly those that would empty the map, without a single exception.

### Changed
- **The year axis and the Isolate button announce themselves.** At rest the axis carries a dotted underline, and on hover the accent colour and a rule: it finally says it can be clicked. The Isolate button is announced before it exists: hovering a player, the panel says a click will pin them and bring up the button; when it appears, a ring marks the spot, twice.
- **A click in empty space no longer releases a pinned team.** A team is picked in the header, outside the area one drags: an unlucky click must not lose it. It is released by clicking its header again, or by the × of the detail panel — two explicit exits, consistent with the isolated edition, which is only left by clicking its year again. Once isolated, empty space had in any case nearly vanished from the screen, the columns being packed together: it was no longer a reliable exit. A **player**, on the other hand, is still released by a click in empty space: they were picked on the map, and the map is their context. A column header now states both gestures in its tooltip.
- **The map opens on the whole period, 2011 → 2026**, and the "All" preset is the one active at start-up. It used to open on 2013 to spare the 500 px of nearly empty rows of Seasons 1 and 2 — three and six teams tracked —, but hiding two editions by default cost more than it gained. Being exhaustive is the point of the tool: narrowing is the period filter's job, as thinning out is the appearances threshold's. All **120** columns are now there on first load, not 110.
- **The two ends of the thread sit on either side of the line between two editions.** The bar marking a first and last Worlds floated at the edge of the five-row block, attached to nothing visible. It now sits **8 px from the year line**, the end above and the start below, and the stem runs up to it: the thread starts or stops between those two years, and it reads without interpretation. The 8 px are not a whim — a career ending and another starting at the next edition fall at the same place, in the same lane if it is the same role, and the two marks merged into one: Dyrus stopping at TSM in 2015, Hauntzer arriving there in 2016. On either side, they are 16 px apart and read as two distinct facts. Above the first displayed row there is no line — the mark then stops at the edge of the world, as the column background already does.
- **The ends of the thread also show when clicking a team, in both modes.** They only appeared in "Full careers". Yet that is where they matter most: an end mark on the row before explains the arrival that follows — the player does not come from nowhere, their predecessor stopped. An end is now highlighted **with its slot**, through the `data-c` it carries, and never with its player: "Arrivals and departures" keeps only some of a player's slots, and a mark attached to an unlit slot floated alone in the middle of the map — Zven carried one at ORI 2015, with TSM pinned.
- **"Arrivals and departures" no longer leaves missing threads.** The mode listed its segments by **career index**, whereas the map draws them between the nodes it kept: as soon as an appearance was left out of the render — by the period, by the filtering of off-column appearances, or by an isolation — the two numberings diverged and the thread stayed unlit. Maple thus arrived at PSG in 2021 without a single line, although that is where he joins TSM from. A segment is now highlighted when **both its slots** are, and a thread end when its slot is: nothing can stay lit with nothing to hang on to, nor unlit for want of a matching index.
- **An isolated team reads player by player, on hover.** The column layout stays the team's, and only the hovered player's line lights up within it: a sub-context, held by the cursor and undone by moving it away, without ever re-laying out the map. The click keeps its meaning — it carries the selection and the isolation over to the player, and then the columns move. Outside isolation a pinned team still ignores hovering, deliberately: otherwise moving the mouse across 120 columns would make the highlight flicker constantly. Isolated, only a handful remain.
- **The click mode also decides which columns isolation keeps.** Switching to "Arrivals and departures" did not re-sort the columns: the map stayed on the previous mode's set. Yet the two modes give very different sets — 27 columns at most in "Full careers", 16 in "Arrivals and departures".
- **A role change reads on the thread.** A segment took the colour of its **arrival** role: the whole path of a jungler turned mid was painted mid, and the colour told the arrival instead of the change. It now carries a gradient that switches sharply at its middle, from the departure role to the arrival role. Five roles make twenty ordered pairs, hence twenty `<linearGradient>` set once for the whole map: in `objectBoundingBox`, coordinates are relative to each path's box, the same gradient serves every segment of the same pair, and there is nothing to compute per segment. Two hard stops at 50% and not a fade — the colour must say "here, they change role", not smear the uncertainty over the whole path. Segments within the same year follow the same rule, without exception: the middle of the path is the middle of the path.
- **The thread's bend adapts to the gap it has to cross.** It was 14 px whatever the offset. For a thread joining the right half-slot of a shared role — sOAZ's case, FNC 2017 then the right half of top in 2018 —, that is **63 px to cross in 14 px of height**, a 4.5:1 ratio: hence the squashed S, stuck above the slot. The shared role only created the offset; the flaw was that the bend did not adapt to it, so the fix does not need to know about half-slots. The bend's height now follows the lateral gap, the same case going to 57 px (1.1:1), capped by the gutter between two edition blocks. **Both** ends are handled: the starting bend suffered from the same constant and merely showed less, except at support, where it had only 4 px below it for the same 63 px offset. A thread with no offset — the general case, a full slot — keeps exactly the same path.
- **The year axis slides along with the filters drawer**, like the search bar and the legend, instead of disappearing underneath: the filters screen used to come without its time reference. It then passes over the first columns, a price accepted on purpose — the map's free overscroll lets them be pulled out from underneath. Below 900 px wide the drawer covers almost the whole screen: nothing to shift, as for the other two. The bar and the legend now sit to the right of the shifted axis, not of the drawer: at `z-index` 20 against 5, they would otherwise have covered 46 px of it — the reference would have been moved only to be hidden behind something else.
- **The README, `CONTRIBUTING.md` and this changelog are in English**, and the README's screenshot is the English map. `docs/SOURCES.md` stays in French, with an English summary at the top. Code comments and the validator's messages are in English; code identifiers stay French, since `docs/SOURCES.md` refers to them by name.

## [1.2.0] — 2026-11-12

### Fixed
- **Clubs crossed outside Worlds have an acronym.** The 37 clubs without a column showed their **full name** in a badge cut for three letters: since the badge does not shrink and the handle is the only thing that does, the player's name vanished into an ellipsis. They now carry their acronym, derived from `Teams.Short` exactly like a column code, since the two are read side by side in "Other teams" — new `clubs` table in `data/careers.json`, computed in § 14.4 of the collection report. All 37 have one, with no collision between them nor with a column code. A club whose source gives no abbreviation would get **none**, and its slot would stay without a badge: a column code is a key and must exist, a club acronym is only a label, and abbreviating a team name oneself would mean writing data the source does not carry.
- **Team tag and `*` / `?` marks stuck back to the right of their slot.** The rule pinning the tag to the tenure kept applying while the tenure was `display:none` — that is, as long as the "Tenure with the team" option stays unticked, so by default. The 511 slots whose player is at their second Worlds with the team and the 22 rotation marks lost their alignment and stuck to the handle, visible mostly in "Other teams", where every slot carries a tag.
- **The two ends of the thread no longer land on the neighbouring slot.** The bar marking a first and last Worlds fell 9 px from the slot's edge, that is **in the row of the role above** — and since the other slots drop to .28 opacity on hover, it read as the neighbouring player's mark. It now sits on the junction between two editions, in the gutter where no slot lives: the two exact points where the lines already attach their vertical path.
- **Organisation lineage cut by a redirect.** `Teams.RenamedTo` does not carry a page title but the name the team continued under, which is sometimes only a redirect: `Kingzone DragonX` is renamed "DragonX", an alias of the `DRX` page. Comparing the raw strings stopped the lineage there. The target is now resolved through `TeamRedirects` before comparing, which attaches eight more steps to `DRX` and corrects two `active` windows along the way: `DNS` to 2014 (`Rebels Anarchy`) and `GAM` to 2015 (`Boba Marines`).
- **"Other regions" column: no more ghost slots.** An off-column appearance now only shows there if it touches the visible map — the edition of departure, that of return, and the clubs a line would otherwise cross silently. Measured over the 31 region combinations: 1,008 slots before, 512 after (−49%). Clicking the player still gives their full career with the exact years, unfiltered.
- **Lines attached under a shared role.** The ten curve lanes are spread over a full slot's width; for top right, jungle right, bot left and support left, the lane fell outside the half-slot and the line seemed to come out from under the neighbouring name — 14 of the 17 shared roles. The line now drops straight under its name and only joins its lane once clear, through a bend. The lane system, which keeps two roles from overlapping, is unchanged; full slots are drawn identically.

### Added
- **The thread no longer skips the years without Worlds.** `data/careers.json` says, for each of the 419 gap years of the 749 players, **where the player was**: 435 steps, 371 of them in an existing column and 64 in "Other teams". It is a new selection rule, distinct from the four Worlds rules — **rule 5**: the year's majority club, plus any other club at 20% of the games or more, rule 1's threshold carried over from the axis of roles to the axis of time. Its check is the row itself: applied to the 1,557 player-years that have a row, it finds the right column **1,549 times**. A step only says "at X that year" — no roster, no role, no rotation — and its slot is hatched, so that the distinction survives any setting. **It only shows when hovering the player or clicking a team**: at rest the thread keeps its usual shortcut, since the 435 slots, and above all the lines they draw towards "Other teams", far right of 120 columns, would cover the map for information read one player at a time. The source giving no role for a step, the step sticks to the starter as a **half-slot**, like a shared role, when its thread's role is already held that year — 18 cases, among them Nagne and PraY at NaJin in 2014, the year Ggoong and Zefa played Worlds there. The starter only shrinks when the step appears: at rest their slot is full, and the Worlds row loses nothing for information nobody asked for.
- **A noise filter taken from the source, never guessed from the name.** Two fields rule out what is not a career step: `Tournaments.TournamentLevel = 'Primary'` for academies, secondary leagues and showmatches (2,567 grouped lines out of 8,259), then the existence of a `Teams` record for national teams — which the tournament level does not catch, the Asian Games qualifiers being `Primary` — the regional selections of the KeSPA Cup and the one-night teams of the Demacia Cup (34 entities). `IsOfficial` was tried and ruled out: the LMS, GPL, CBLOL and the pre-2017 LPL are not marked in it, and it would empty 144 player-years for 2016 alone. The explicit table provided for the rest is **empty**.
- **The two ends of the thread, on hover**: a start mark at the player's first Worlds, an end mark at their last, and the detail panel gives the **first and last known appearance** — which are not the same things: 567 players have a game recorded after their last Worlds. The wording stops where the source stops: "known appearance", never "debut" or "end of career"; "no professional game on record", never "no team". Over a span where nothing is recorded — seven cases — the thread turns dotted on hover.
- **Every team that played a Worlds has its column: 54 more, 120 in all, 300 rows.** Not a single team of the sixteen editions is out of scope any more — § 10 of the collection report is empty. Nine region families are born, all named after a league like the original five: `CBLOL` (10 columns), `VCS` (8), `LCP` (6), `TCL` (6), `GPL` (6), `LLA` (5), `LCO` (5), `LCL` (4), `LJL` (4). Three decisions are worth knowing: the LLA and Oceania stay **separate** — they share neither a league nor a territory —, the 2025 LTA South succeeds neither the CBLOL nor the LLA and its only row is settled by the organisation's location, and the nine `homeCountries` lists are decided one by one, since a forgotten code silently turns every player from that country into an import.
- **"Biggest travellers" becomes two statistics**, because it was two: the teams **the player went to Worlds with**, and every club crossed over their career, steps outside Worlds and clubs without a column included. The former title said "among those tracked" without saying which of the two it counted.
- **"Worlds appearances" filter**, four levels — `All · 2+ · 3+ · 5+` — giving 120, 54, 33 and 19 columns. A single bound and not two: a period is an interval, a number of appearances is a threshold. Levels rather than a slider: 87 columns out of 120 have one or two appearances, and a slider from 1 to 13 would spend half its travel moving nothing. It adds up with the region and period filters and with hiding columns one by one — a team it rules out flows into "Other teams" like any hidden column. Region pills show their count as soon as a threshold rules columns out, so that an empty region is not empty in silence.
- **The scope rule becomes executable.** `fetch-worlds.mjs` computes each row's region from the league actually played that year, on five levels of evidence — the Worlds regional qualifier, a `Primary`-level domestic league, the dominant territory over `Primary` leagues, the territory across all levels confirmed by `Teams.Region`, then `Teams.Region` alone. § 13 of the report compares the computation with the `PLAN_REGIONS` table: all **300 rows** are reproduced, and only four rest on the last resort. Two Leaguepedia regions are not regions and are settled by the organisation's location: `SEA`, which runs from Taiwan to Vietnam, and `Americas`, that of the 2025 LTA South.
- **The dataset's first handle collision, handled.** Two people go by `Violet` and are now both shown — Saigon Jokers 2012 and PEACE 2021. Their identities get the disambiguator of their Leaguepedia page; their slots show `Violet`, the handle both of them wore. The disambiguator is an identity, not a label.
- **The five major regions are complete: 33 more columns, 66 in all, 215 rows.** They come out of § 10 of the collection report — teams met outside the scope — through a new scope rule: **a row enters the map if the organisation played, in that edition's year, one of the five tracked leagues**, under the name it then bore. `Teams.Region` is not authoritative, since it gives today's region: it files 100 Thieves and KaBuM! under the same "Americas" label and has EMEA swallow Turkey. Scope is therefore decided row by row. Applied to the 170 rows already entered, the rule **reproduces them all**. Of the 89 out-of-scope organisations, 33 come in and 56 remain for the minor regions. By region: `AZF GRF DNS` in the LCK, `LNG WBG WE SN IM` in the LPL, `RGE SK BDS AAA GMB MSF LD ALL VIT GDE M5` in the LEC, `100T EG CG LYON NRG VUL IMT EPIK LMQ DIG` in the LCS, `HKA GRX MAD GAB` in the LMS. No new concept: same five regions, same palette, same pipeline.
- **The `active` windows are computed**, no longer set from memory: the organisation's first and last game on Leaguepedia (`ScoreboardGames`), all competitions included, over its whole lineage of pages — aliases from `TeamRedirects`, predecessors from `Teams.RenamedTo`, and the `LIGNEES_ARBITREES` table for what neither links. The end bound is only set if *all* these pages carry `IsDisbanded`. Of the 33 existing columns, the computation reproduces 29 and corrects the other four: `KT` to 2012 (`KT Rolster Bullets`), `TES` to 2017 (`Topsports Gaming`), `KC` to 2020 (`Kameto Corp`), `TL` to 2012 (`Team Curse`).
- **The results in `data/results.json` are computed** from `TournamentResults.Place`: 1 → `W`, 2 → `F`, 3-4 → `SF`, 5-8 → `QF`, then `P` if all the team's scoreboards are on the `…/Play-In` page, otherwise `G` or `S` depending on whether the Main Event rounds are called `Day 1`…`Day 8` or `Round 1`…`Round 5`. The computation reproduces all 215 results without exception; only `F*` — qualified for a final not yet played — is still set by hand, Leaguepedia leaving those two places at `null`.
- **The 2011 and 2012 Worlds enter the map**, which now covers **16 editions**. Both Seasons are derived from the same single source and the same four rules as the others, without changing a line of the pipeline: 280 and 310 scoreboard lines, complete roles, 3 then 6 teams tracked, 9 new rows and 26 nationalities. The "All" preset goes down to 2011, but the map **still opens on 2013 → 2026**: those two rows are very sparse.
- **`CLGEU` column ("CLG EU")**, the map's 33rd. The CLG organisation fielded two teams in Season 2: `TeamRedirects` brings `CLG Prime` back to the `Counter Logic Gaming` org page, which therefore keeps the `CLG` column, while `Counter Logic Gaming Europe` has its own org page and gets its own — the same treatment as Samsung White / Samsung Blue in 2014. `active [2012, 2012]` window, the organisation having disbanded.
- **`fetch-worlds.mjs` names the regional qualifiers living under an edition's prefix**, and fails if one of them carries games. `Season 1 World Championship/Qualifiers/Europe` and `/North America` are the only two in the dataset; they carry no scoreboard line, but the exclusion no longer rests on that chance. The discriminator is the region: an edition page and its stages are "International".
- **Hiding and showing a team column.** A `×` appears when hovering the header, and the filters drawer gets a checklist grouped by region, with "all / none" per group and a "show all again" — the checklist is the source of truth, the `×` only a shortcut to it. A hidden team's players go through "Other teams", exactly like those of a hidden region. The two filters **add up**: hiding teams acts within the displayed regions. The last column cannot be hidden.
- **Three header states when clicking a team**: the pinned column gets an accent ring, those that exchanged players with it stay normal, the others are greyed out. Over the 31 neighbouring columns, 82% are greyed out in "Full careers" mode and 86% in "Arrivals and departures". The "linked" set is derived from the slots actually highlighted, so it agrees with the displayed period; the "Other teams" header is linked as soon as it carries one. Hovering no longer shares the pinned column's style.
- **Country name spelled out** in the detail panel ("South Korea" and not `KR`), through a `countryNames` table in `data/players.json`. The slots' badge keeps the two-letter code, for lack of room. `npm run validate` now rejects a nationality code without a name.

### Changed
- **The legend is closed at start-up**, at every width and no longer only below 700 px. At ten lines it covered the bottom left of the map from the start, for content one reads once.
- **`fetch-worlds.mjs` writes the `split` field into its fragments**, and `findDiffs` really compares it. The 23 roles actually split into two half-slots used to be listed as still to convert, although they were already in the data: § 7.5 of the report is now empty, and the divergence counter only carries the deliberate trace of team names (§ 7.6).
- **Nationalities rest on `Players.NationalityPrimary`**, no longer on `Players.Country`, the country of birth. The rule was written in `docs/SOURCES.md` but not tooled: the script now reads both fields. The 362 nationalities entered are **all confirmed**; the 119 new ones come from it, which changes `Dan Dinh` (born in the Philippines, American), `nuc` (born in Morocco, French) and `Dhokla` (born in India, American). `homeCountries.LEC` gains `RU`, `AM` and `EE`: the Moscow Five and Gambit Gaming rosters played the European circuit at a time when the CIS did not yet have a league.
- **Free overscroll of the map**: the drag margin beyond the edges goes from a fixed `160 px` to `max(320px, 50% of the viewport)`, enough to pull the first column out from under the filters drawer. "Show all" remains the safety net.
- **The search bar and the legend slide to the right of the filters drawer** when it opens, instead of passing under it (above 900 px wide). The legend's chevrons are flipped: it unfolds upwards.
- **The "Other regions" column becomes "Other teams"**, since it now also takes the teams hidden one by one. Its subtitle lists the hidden regions, then the number of hidden teams.
- **"Other teams" column arranged by role**: the row comes from the role, as in a team column, instead of a rank in the list — the slot no longer jumps rows from one year to the next and the thread no longer zigzags. Its width adapts to the per-role total.

## [1.1.0] — 2026-11-11

### Added
- `scripts/fetch-worlds.mjs`: collects the starters of the 14 editions from Leaguepedia, applies the selection rules, and reports the cases to settle.
- **LMS** region and 11 historical columns: TSM, ahq, CLG, Flash Wolves, OMG, NaJin, H2K, Origen, LGD, Taipei Assassins, Samsung Blue.
- Editions **2013 to 2016**: 46 rows, 4 standings, 111 nationalities.
- Nine rows that were missing from 2017–2026: TSM 2017 and 2020, ahq 2017 and 2019, Flash Wolves 2017 and 2018, Splyce 2019, J Team 2019, LGD 2020.
- Six rotations that were missing from 2017–2026: Haru, Rascal, Ambition, Duke, Deftly, Burdol.
- `active` field on each team (the organisation's lifetime), `aliases` (handle worn that year) and `split` (roles shown as half-slots) fields per row.
- **Period filter**: two year lists and three presets (All, Modern era 2020→, Historic era 2013–2019). The cut falls in 2020, the year the LMS stops being a major region — the dataset's sharpest break, where 2017 was on the contrary its most continuous transition.
- **Shared roles in two half-slots**: each one can be targeted and hovered and links its own career; the two threads of the same role follow distinct lanes.
